Projects/Community protection

A referral pathway run by residents themselves

Four camps, and one question I asked every committee: who do you go to when something happens?

Area
Humanitarian work
Service
Humanitarian protection
Goal
A way to report that residents trust
Client
International organization
My role
Lead consultant
Duration
14 months · 2022–2023

The story

Situation

Four camps, home to about twelve thousand people. The organization had a reporting system, a phone line and an office, but people did not use it. Women in particular did not trust that what they said would stay between them and whoever listened.

What I did

I sat with each of the four camp committees separately, before any meeting with the organization. I asked one question: who do you go to when something happens? The answer was always a neighbour, an elder or a teacher. So we designed a pathway together that starts with them, and trained forty of them on what to say, what not to say, and where to refer.

What changed

A year later, most reports arrived through local committees, not the organization's offices. The time from report to response fell to about a third. Most important: the committees now run the pathway with no staff from the organization.

Why I took this project

Because no one had asked the residents before. Every system had been designed for the organization's offices, not for them.

— Ahmed

How the work went

Fourteen months, in order.

  1. Months 1–2Listening sessions with each of the four committees.
  2. Month 3Two workshops designing the pathway with residents and the organization.
  3. Months 4–6Training 40 volunteers, and testing the pathway in one camp.
  4. Months 7–12Extending to all four camps, with a review every three months.
  5. Months 13–14Handing management to the committees, and an independent evaluation.
